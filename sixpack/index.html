Equipment setup

High pulley

Lat pulldown, face pull, triceps pushdown, kneeling cable crunch.

Chest-height pulley

Chest press, cable row, Pallof press and woodchop variations.

Low pulley

Biceps curls, hammer curls, lateral raises and glute kickbacks.

Attachments

D-handle for single-arm work; rope for face pulls, pushdowns and hammer curls; ankle strap for kickbacks.

Use a stable stance and check that attachments are locked in. If your machine has only one pulley, do one-sided exercises one side at a time.
Recovery & stamina

Easy aerobic work

On recovery days, take a 20–30 minute brisk but comfortable walk. Build duration gradually.

Pelvic-floor awareness

Practice gentle contraction followed by complete relaxation. Do not hold your breath or strain.

Breathing reset

For 1–2 minutes, breathe slowly into the lower ribs and abdomen, relaxing the pelvic floor as you inhale.

Mobility

Gentle hip-flexor, chest and upper-back mobility for 5–10 minutes as comfortable.

Fitness supports general cardiovascular health, but this routine cannot guarantee changes in sexual performance. Seek clinical advice for persistent erectile changes, pelvic pain or other symptoms.
Progress & fat loss
  • Week 1: Choose repeatable loads; leave 2–3 reps in reserve.
  • Week 2: Add 1–2 reps to sets while keeping good form.
  • Week 3: When all reps are comfortable, add a small amount of resistance.
  • Week 4: Maintain the load or reduce sets slightly if tired. Then repeat the cycle.
  • Ab exercises strengthen the core; they do not selectively burn belly fat.
  • Visible abs depend on overall body-fat level, muscle, genetics and nutrition.
  • Use a modest, sustainable calorie deficit and include protein at meals.
  • Track waist measurement, strength and consistency, not only scale weight.
Training checklist

Before

Warm up 3–4 min · check cable and attachments · choose a controlled load

During

Smooth reps · breathe continuously · rest 30–60 sec as needed

After

Cool down briefly · note weights and reps · recover, hydrate and sleep

Adjust

Reduce load if form slips · use an easier variation if needed · stop for sharp or unusual pain

General fitness guidance, not a medical assessment. If you have a chronic condition, significant symptoms or have been advised to restrict exercise, check with your healthcare professional before starting.
